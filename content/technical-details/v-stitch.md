The V-stitch is one of the most useful stitches for shawls. Each "V" is two tall stitches worked into the same place with a chain between them, which opens the fabric and gives it a lovely, fluid drape.

## The structure

In UK terms, each V is worked as (tr, ch 1, tr) into the same stitch. In US terms the same thing is written (dc, ch 1, dc).

On the first row, the Vs are spaced out along the foundation chain, usually by skipping two chains between each one. On every row after that, each new V is worked into the ch-1 space at the centre of the V below. That stacking is what creates the neat, vertical columns.

## Why it suits shawls

- It grows quickly, so a large piece does not take forever.
- The open fabric is light on the shoulders, even in a warm wool.
- It shows off gradient and self-striping yarns beautifully.

## Things to watch

Count your Vs at the end of each row. Missing the centre space and working into a stitch instead is the most common mistake, and it quietly pulls the edges out of line.
