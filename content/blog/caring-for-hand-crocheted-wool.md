Crochet fabric is generous and open, which is what makes it drape so beautifully. It also means it needs a little more gentleness than a machine-knitted jumper. The good news: caring for it takes very little time.

## Wash less than you think

Wool is naturally resistant to odours. Most of the time, airing a shawl over a chair back overnight is all it needs. When it does need a wash, once or twice a season is plenty.

## The gentle wash

- Fill a basin with cool or lukewarm water and a small amount of wool wash.
- Submerge the piece and press it down gently. Do not rub, twist or wring.
- Leave it to soak for around fifteen minutes, then rinse in water of the same temperature.
- Press out the water, then roll the piece in a clean towel to lift out more.

## Dry it flat

Lay the piece flat on a dry towel or a drying rack, away from direct heat and strong sunlight. Ease it back into shape with your hands while it is damp. Hanging a wet crochet piece lets the weight of the water stretch it out of shape.

## Storing between seasons

Fold, rather than hang, your shawls and scarves. Store them somewhere dry, ideally with a cedar block or a lavender sachet, which moths dislike.
